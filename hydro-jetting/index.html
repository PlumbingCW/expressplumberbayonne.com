Sewer backup: when jetting is the right call.

A cable breaks through a clog. A hydro jet scours the pipe wall, which is why it is the better choice for a backup that keeps returning. A nozzle on a flexible hose sends water jets forward to open the line and backward to pull the hose along, flushing debris to the cleanout.

Call the emergency line when sewage is backing up, when several fixtures drain slowly together, or when a cabling visit gave only a few weeks of relief. Jetting needs access to a cleanout, and the plumber confirms the pipe is sound before using it.

Grease buildup.

Kitchen and restaurant lines coat with fat that cooling turns into a hard layer.

Scale and mineral deposits.

Older iron and steel pipe develops a crust that narrows the passage.

Roots.

Fine roots enter at joints and grow into a mat that catches paper and waste.

Sludge in low spots.

A sagging section holds sediment that cabling only moves around.

Jetting is a cleaning method and not a code standard, but the camera pass that goes with it can reveal pipe that must be repaired under the state plumbing subcode. Ask the plumber to show you the video and explain any finding.
