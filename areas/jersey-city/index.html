Jersey City water: what the utility says and what it means for your fixtures.

Jersey City's water comes from the Boonton Reservoir and the Split Rock Reservoir, according to the Jersey City Municipal Utilities Authority's water quality report. In the 2018 report the hardness ranged from 60 to 97 parts per million, which is about 3.5 to 5.7 grains per gallon. That is a moderate level, and the current report is the one to check for today's figures.

Moderate hardness leaves scale slowly. It matters most for tankless heaters, which narrow with scale inside the heat exchanger, and for tank heaters that have never been flushed. If you run a tankless water heater, a yearly flush is a reasonable schedule until you know your own number.

The same report says the utility adds corrosion control chemicals to reduce lead and copper dissolving from household plumbing. It also advises running a tap for 30 seconds to two minutes if water has sat in the pipes for hours. Older buildings may have lead or galvanized service lines, and New Jersey law requires public water systems to replace known lead and galvanized lines. Ask your utility what your line is made of.

If you have a burst pipe or active leak in Jersey City: before we arrive.

Shut off the main.

In a Jersey City multi-family building the shutoff may be in a shared basement, a hallway closet or behind a locked door. Call the super or landlord while you call us.

Kill power near water.

Switch off the breaker to the wet area only if you can reach it without standing in water. Stay clear of outlets and the panel.

Clear the area.

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the path. If water is coming through a ceiling, place buckets and punch a small hole only if a plumber advises it.

Call the 24/7 line.

Give the dispatcher your address, ZIP code and floor, and tell us whether other units are affected.

Then photograph the damage before you clean up. Tenants and owners both need the record for insurance and for the building manager.
