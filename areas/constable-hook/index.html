Plumber in Constable Hook, Bayonne, NJ

Constable Hook is the southern peninsula of Bayonne, a mix of homes, apartment buildings and industrial land. Bayonne Express Plumbing handles both residential and small commercial plumbing calls here.

The mix is the reason this page exists. A call from a two-family house and a call from a shop or warehouse need different questions, and we ask them up front.

A neighborhood with an old name.

Wikipedia says Constable Hook takes its name from a 1646 land grant to the chief gunner at Fort Amsterdam. The same article mentions two burial grounds, tank farms, and the Bayonne Golf Club, which it places at the city's highest point.

That tells you something practical. Constable Hook is not one kind of place. It has industrial land and residential blocks side by side, and we treat each call by the kind of building it comes from.

Bayonne ZIP code 07002 covers the whole city. See our page for plumbing in Bayonne for the other sections.

What a local real estate guide says about the area.

A Bayonne real estate guide describes Constable Hook as an emerging waterfront area with converted industrial-style lofts and newer developments. NeighborhoodScout, a third-party data site, describes apartment complexes and small buildings here, with many built between 1970 and 1999 in part of the area.

Both are secondary sources. We use them only to say that the housing here is mixed. They do not tell us what pipes are in a particular building, and we do not guess.

Newer buildings.

Newer construction can still have a failed water heater or a slow drain. See water heater replacement.

Older small buildings.

Small older buildings may have had piecemeal repairs, so a sewer camera inspection shows what a drain line really looks like.

Commercial spaces.

Floor drains, restrooms and shared sewer lines are the usual trouble spots. See commercial plumbing.

Practical points for owners and managers.

  • Know where the main water shutoff is and tell everyone who works or lives in the building..
  • Keep the property manager's number handy so a tenant can report a leak quickly..
  • Photograph damage before cleanup, since an insurer will ask for it..
  • For a gas smell, leave the building, go about 350 feet away, then call PSE&G at 1-800-880-7734 or 911..

A plumber can fix a leaking pipe, but a gas leak belongs to the utility and the fire department first. The PSE&G guidance above is the utility's own instruction.

Plumbing in New Jersey follows N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which adopts the IAPMO National Standard Plumbing Code with state amendments. A licensed master plumber must do the work or supervise it.
