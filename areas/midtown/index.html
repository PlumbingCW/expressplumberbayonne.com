Broadway and the streets around it.

A Bayonne real estate guide describes Midtown as running along Broadway, with mixed-use buildings and transit-oriented development. Wikipedia places the Bayonne Town Center in the Broadway shopping district and names William Shemin Midtown Community School No. 8.

The Hudson-Bergen Light Rail stops at 45th Street, 34th Street and 22nd Street, each just east of Avenue E. The guide gives no exact Midtown boundary, so we do not draw one. If you are on or near Broadway, call and give the address.

Why shared buildings change the plumbing call.

In a building with a store at street level and apartments above, drains, risers and water lines are often shared. A clog at the restaurant sink can show up as slow drains upstairs, and a leak upstairs can drip into the shop.

NeighborhoodScout, a third-party site, reports that about 40 percent of housing in the City Center area is in two to four unit buildings and that many were built before 1940. It is an estimate and it does not tell us what any one building contains.

When we arrive we trace the problem from the affected fixture back toward the shared line. That shows whether the repair belongs to one unit or to the whole building.

Work on Avenue C and what to expect.

PSE&G is replacing cast iron gas pipe in Bayonne, including Avenue C from 21st Street to 53rd Street. That is gas main work and it is separate from plumbing. A gas smell is a PSE&G and 911 matter first, never a plumbing call.

Permits for plumbing work go through the Bayonne building department at 630 Avenue C. The city expects a permit to be filed within 72 hours after emergency work, and the work itself must be done by a licensed master plumber or under one. The state plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, adopts the IAPMO National Standard Plumbing Code with New Jersey amendments.

When a shared line backs up.

  • Tell the other units and the manager right away so no one runs water.
  • Note which fixtures are slow and on which floors.
  • Call us with that list, since it points to where the blockage sits.
