Hoboken in a storm: sewers, basements and sump pumps.

Hoboken has a combined sewer system, where rainwater and wastewater share the same pipes. The City of Hoboken and the North Hudson Sewerage Authority have said that as little as 0.8 inches of rain per hour can overwhelm the system. In April 2025 they announced five new stormwater pumps at ResilienCity Park to reduce flooding and overflows.

That matters inside a building. When the street system fills, a floor drain or a basement toilet can back up. A sump pump and a backwater valve keep sewage and stormwater out of the basement, and a plumber can check both before the next hard rain. See basement waterproofing for the plumbing options.

If your drain backs up only in heavy rain, tell the plumber and the sewerage authority. The cause may be capacity in the street, and cabling the line will not fix it. See drain cleaning for what a line cleaning can and cannot do.

Who owns the water line in Hoboken.

The City of Hoboken owns the water system and Veolia operates it. Hoboken buys its water from the Jersey City Municipal Utilities Authority. According to the city, the property owner owns the service line from the water main to the meter inside the home, and the city is responsible for the main, typically in the street.

The city also says most pipes in the system are 100 years old or older, with more than 41 miles of water main.

It says there are no lead water mains in Hoboken, but properties built before 1986 could have service lines made of materials that contain lead, including galvanized steel. The city's lead service line program aims to replace all service lines by 2031 and does the work at no direct cost to owners when it is working on their block.

Practically, that means a service line leak between the main and your meter is yours to repair. A plumber can find it with a meter test, and water service line repair explains the options. Tell the plumber if your block is scheduled for city work, so a repair is not duplicated.
