Water heater emergency: leak or no hot water.

A water heater repair starts with finding which part failed and whether the tank is still sound. Burner and heating element faults, thermostats, gas valves, pilot assemblies, relief valves and drain valves are all repairable. A tank that leaks from its body is not, because the steel shell has failed.

Treat the call as an emergency when the tank is leaking, when you smell gas near the heater, when the relief valve is discharging steadily, or when there is no hot water and the weather is cold. Close the cold supply valve above the heater and call.

Water at the base.

A drip from a fitting or a drain valve can be repaired. Water from the tank body usually means replacement.

No hot water at all.

Electric heaters often have a failed element or thermostat. Gas units may have a pilot or gas valve fault.

Not enough hot water.

A broken dip tube or sediment layer can cut the usable hot water in half.

Rumbling or popping.

Sediment baked onto the bottom of the tank. Flushing can help if the tank is otherwise sound.

According to the state plumbing subcode, hot water is defined as 120 to 140 degrees Fahrenheit, so a heater set outside that range deserves a look. Gas work on a heater is plumbing work under the master plumber rule, and a permit may apply when parts are replaced.

Gas valve, pilot and thermostat faults.

On a gas water heater, the gas control valve combines the thermostat, pilot safety and burner supply. When the pilot will not stay lit or the burner never fires, the thermocouple or the valve is often the cause. Replacing the valve is a gas job and belongs to a licensed plumber.

Electric heaters use two heating elements and thermostats. A plumber tests each one with a meter, replaces the failed part and flushes sediment if the tank is clogged. See our guide on when a repair stops making sense and replacement costs less.

Bayonne water, winter demand and your heater.

In January 2026 a damaged 30-inch water main in South Kearny dropped pressure across Bayonne and the city issued a boil water advisory that ran from January 22 to January 24. When the advisory lifted, the city suggested draining and refilling water heaters set below 113 degrees Fahrenheit and running faucets for three to five minutes.

The practical point is simple. A pressure drop can stir sediment loose, and a heater that has sat unused may need a flush. If your heater made new noises or gave rusty water after that week, have it checked before the next cold snap.

In a tight basement the heater may sit close to a boiler, a washer or a floor drain. A leak there spreads fast, so know where the cold water shutoff is and close it before you call.

Insurance note.

A sudden tank failure that floods a finished basement may be covered as accidental water damage, while a slow leak or wear usually is not. Read your policy and keep photos and the old parts.

What to do before the plumber arrives.

  • Close the cold water valve on the pipe above the heater if water is leaking..
  • For gas units, turn the gas control knob to off. If you smell gas, leave the building and call PSE&G at 1-800-880-7734 or 911 from outside..
  • Switch off the breaker to an electric heater if it is leaking, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water..
  • Place a bucket or towels under a slow drip and photograph the unit, the label and the leak..
