How to check a plumber's license in New Jersey.

New Jersey's State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers says plumbing is to be done by a master plumber, an authorized plumbing contractor or their employees. That applies to us and to every other plumber you hire.

  • Ask the plumber for the license number before work starts.
  • Look the number up with the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs.
  • Ask for a written scope and price for the job.
  • Be wary of anyone who cannot answer these questions on the phone.

What a clear price means here.

Plumbing prices depend on the problem, the parts and the access. We do not guess a number on the phone for a problem nobody has seen. The plumber diagnoses on site, explains the repair options and quotes a price before any work begins.

You can say no. If you want a second opinion, the plumber explains what they found so another company can look at the same thing.

What we cover and what we do not.

We cover emergency calls, repairs, replacements and installations in Bayonne, Jersey City and Hoboken. Gas utility work, street water mains and public sewers belong to the utility or the city. For a street main problem, Veolia operates the water and sewer system in Bayonne, and a plumber cannot work on it.

If your problem sits across that line, the dispatcher will tell you who to call for each part. That saves you a wasted visit.

Why a Bayonne base matters.

Bayonne is a peninsula with one main road spine and a handful of bridges and approaches. A plumber who works from Broadway knows how long the trip to Constable Hook or Bergen Point takes at different hours, and where parking is tight near older multi-family houses.

That local habit shapes the call. We ask for the cross street, whether the shutoff is in a basement and whether the building is a one-family house, a two-family or a shop. It sounds small, and it saves a second trip for parts.

Homes close to the water also have their own pattern. Basements near Newark Bay or the Kill Van Kull can take on water during storms, so we ask about sump pumps and floor drains early in the call.

What our guides and pages are based on.

We write the service pages and guides from city notices, utility pages and state licensing information, and we leave out anything we cannot source. Where a fact depends on your address, such as a permit rule or a meter location, the page tells you who to ask.

We do not publish license numbers, reviews or ratings on this site. If you want to confirm a plumber's license, the steps above show how.

Homes, rentals and shops.

Many Bayonne buildings are two-family houses or apartments, so a plumbing problem often involves a landlord, a neighbor or a shared shutoff. We ask who controls the building valves and who needs to approve the work, so the visit is not wasted at the door.

Shops and restaurants get the same line. We ask about grease traps, floor drains and shared sewer connections up front.

After-hours calls.

Nights, weekends and holidays get the same answer as a weekday morning. The dispatcher asks what is happening, tells you what to shut off while you wait and books the next available appointment for your address.

Appointments are same-day when available. We do not promise an arrival time on the phone, because traffic, the job before yours and the size of the problem all change it. The dispatcher tells you what is known when you call.
