Jammed, humming or leaking disposal: what to do first.

A garbage disposal grinds food with a spinning plate and flings it into a drain line. When it hums but does not spin, a hard object has jammed the plate. When it is silent, the motor, the reset button or the power may have failed. When water appears under the sink, a seal or the sink flange has leaked.

Switch the unit off at the wall and never put your hand in. Look underneath for water. If it is leaking, close the cold supply valve under the sink and place a bucket. Call us for a leak or a backup, because standing water in the sink is a clog downstream.

Humming but not turning.

A jam. The plumber frees the plate from below with the correct wrench.

Silent and dead.

Check the reset button on the bottom. If it will not reset, the motor or wiring may have failed.

Leak from the side or bottom.

A cracked housing or failed seal means the unit needs replacing.

Water backing up in the sink.

A clog in the drain line below the disposal, often grease and ground food.

Following the state standard, disposals connect to the drain and to the electrical system, so the state plumbing subcode and electrical rules both apply to a new install. A repair of a jammed or leaking unit is maintenance. Ask a licensed plumber about a new circuit.

Repair, replace or leave it out.

A jam is usually a quick fix. A leak from the housing is not repairable, and a dead motor on an old unit often costs more to fix than to replace. The plumber tells you which makes sense. See drain cleaning if the sink drain is the real problem.

Kitchens in Bayonne: grease and shared drains.

Grease is the most common cause of a clog below a disposal. A disposal does not break up fat, which cools in the pipe and sticks. If your kitchen sink backs up often, ask about hydro jetting for the line behind the wall.

In a multi-family building the kitchen stack may be shared. A clog downstream of your disposal may show up in another unit, so tell the building owner or manager and the plumber what you see.

Bayonne sewers are operated by Veolia for the city. A backup that affects only your kitchen is on your side. If neighbors report the same problem, call Veolia as well.

Using a disposal without trouble.

  • Run cold water before, during and after use..
  • Skip grease, coffee grounds in bulk, fibrous peels, bones and pasta in large amounts..
  • Never pour chemical drain cleaner into a sink with a disposal..
  • Grind small amounts at a time, not a whole pot of scraps..
