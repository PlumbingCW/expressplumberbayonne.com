Sewer backup: what to do first.

A sewer line repair fixes the private pipe that carries waste from your house to the street connection. Common failures include cracks, offset joints, collapsed sections, sags that hold water, root intrusion and old pipe that has corroded or scaled over.

Treat the call as an emergency when sewage is backing up through a floor drain, tub or toilet, when several fixtures drain slowly at once, or when you smell sewer gas in the basement. Stop using water, because every flush adds to the backup.

Backups in the lowest drain.

Sewage rises first at the basement floor drain or the lowest tub.

Repeated clogs.

A line that clogs every few months usually has a defect that cabling cannot cure.

Sewer smell or wet ground.

A break leaks sewage into the soil and can show as damp patches or a stain near the foundation.

Slow drains everywhere.

When every fixture slows together, the main line is restricted or damaged.

Sewer repairs are regulated work. According to the state plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, applies, and digging in the street or sidewalk needs approval from the city. Veolia operates the sewer system in Bayonne, so ask it where the public line begins.

Finding the break before anyone digs.

A repair starts with a camera. The plumber feeds a small camera through the cleanout, records the pipe and notes the distance to each defect. A locator at the surface marks the spot, so the repair opens one small area and not the whole yard or floor.

The plumber then explains the options with the video on screen. See our sewer camera inspection page for how the camera work is done, and sewer line cleaning when the line is sound but blocked.

Pipe lining is also called cured-in-place repair. A resin-soaked liner is pulled through the old pipe and cured to form a new pipe inside it. It avoids a long trench, but the plumber has to confirm the pipe is in condition for it.
