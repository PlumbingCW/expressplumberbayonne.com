How to Install a Sink Garbage Disposal in Bayonne, NJ

Replacing a garbage disposal is one of the more doable plumbing jobs, because the new unit mounts on the same sink flange as the old one. Adding one where there was none is harder, because it needs a drain connection and power.

This guide walks through the steps, points out where leaks and jams start, and says when a plumber should take over.

Before you start: what you need and what to check.

Switch off power to the disposal at the breaker, not just the wall switch. Confirm with a tester that the wires are dead. Gather a screwdriver, pliers, plumber's putty, a new mounting kit if the old one is worn, a bucket and the new unit with its instructions.

Check three things first. The sink needs a standard drain opening. There must be a dishwasher drain hose connection if you have a dishwasher. And the power must come from a switch and an outlet or a hardwired connection, installed safely. If any of those is missing or doubtful, call a plumber and an electrician.

  • Power off at the breaker and tested dead..
  • Bucket and towels under the sink..
  • New unit matches the old mounting style or comes with its own kit..
  • Dishwasher knockout plug removed if the dishwasher will connect to the disposal..
  • Plumber's putty and gloves ready..

Installing the disposal, step by step.

  1. Disconnect the drain trap and the dishwasher hose from the old unit, then unmount it by twisting the lower ring. Support its weight.
  2. Remove the old sink flange by loosening the mounting ring and snap ring, and clean the sink opening of old putty.
  3. Roll a rope of plumber's putty and press it under the new flange. Set the flange in the sink opening.
  4. From below, fit the gasket, backup ring and mounting ring, then tighten the screws evenly to squeeze the putty out. Wipe the excess.
  5. Connect the electrical cord or the hardwired leads following the maker's instructions.
  6. Lift the disposal onto the mounting ring and twist to lock it. Connect the discharge pipe to the trap and the dishwasher hose to the inlet.
  7. Restore power, run water and check every joint for drips. Run the unit for the first time with plenty of cold water.

Leaks usually come from an uneven mounting ring, a missing gasket or a dishwasher hose clamp that is not tight. Fix a leak by loosening, re-seating and tightening evenly, never by adding more putty.

What goes wrong and what it means.

Problem.Likely cause.Fix.
Leak at the sink flange.Putty seal not fully set or uneven ring.Remove, re-putty and retighten.
Leak where the disposal meets the trap.Loose slip nut or worn washer.Tighten or replace washer.
Unit hums and will not spin.Jam.Free the plate from below with the correct wrench.
Sink drains slowly.Clog below the disposal.Cable the drain line.
Dishwasher backs up.Knockout plug left in.Remove the plug.

If the sink backs up after the install, the problem is probably downstream of the unit, and a drain cleaning fixes it. For a unit that leaks from its housing, see garbage disposal repair, because that cannot be tightened away.

When to call a plumber instead.

  • The sink has no standard drain opening or the cabinet space is cramped.
  • There is no nearby power, or the wiring looks worn or unprotected.
  • The drain line is old, corroded or sloped wrongly.
  • You need to relocate the trap or add a dishwasher connection.
  • A first attempt leaked and you do not want to risk the cabinet floor.

A plumber can finish the job in a short visit and test it before leaving.

Choosing a disposal that suits the sink.

Disposals are sold by motor size and by feed type. A continuous feed unit runs while a switch is on. A batch feed unit runs only with the stopper in place, which some households prefer for safety.

Feature.What it means.Who it suits.
Smaller motor.Handles soft scraps.Light use in a small household.
Larger motor.Grinds tougher scraps with less jamming.Heavy cooking and larger households.
Batch feed.Runs only with the stopper set.Homes with children.
Continuous feed.Runs on a switch.Quick everyday use.

Check the height under the sink before you buy. Some units leave little room for the trap, which matters in a shallow cabinet.

Care habits that keep a disposal working.

  • Run cold water before, during and for about a minute after grinding..
  • Feed scraps in small amounts instead of one large load..
  • Keep grease, oil and fat out. They coat the drain downstream..
  • Skip stringy or fibrous scraps such as celery and corn husks..
  • Never put your hand inside. Use tongs for a stuck item after power is off..

Grinding a few ice cubes now and then does not sharpen anything, but it does no harm. A lemon rind can freshen the smell. Neither replaces a periodic clean of the drain line.
