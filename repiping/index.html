When repiping makes more sense than another repair.

Repiping replaces the water supply lines in a home, from the main to the fixtures. It does not usually replace the drains. The plumber removes old pipe and runs new pipe, then reconnects fixtures and tests the system.

The signs that point to repiping are patterns, not single events. A third pinhole leak in a year, rust-colored water from several taps, falling pressure and visible corrosion at joints all say the pipe is wearing out. A single leak in an otherwise good system usually needs a spot repair.

Galvanized pipe.

Steel pipe with a zinc coating corrodes inside, narrows and drops pressure over time.

Pinhole leaks.

Small leaks in copper show up in several spots, which suggests the wall is thinning.

Rusty or brown water.

Corroded pipe sheds rust, especially after the water sits.

Falling pressure.

Scale and corrosion shrink the pipe bore.

Replacing supply lines is permitted work according to the state plumbing subcode. The Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers requires a licensed plumber, and the building department at 630 Avenue C issues the permit and schedules inspection.

Older buildings in Hoboken with galvanized or lead service lines are a common reason owners ask about repiping.

PEX or copper: the choice, in plain terms.

Material.Strengths.Trade-offs.
PEX.Flexible, runs through walls with fewer cuts, faster install.Not for direct sunlight, needs the right fittings.
Copper.Long record, rigid, handles heat well.Costs more, more joints, can pinhole in some water.
CPVC.Lower cost, rigid.Can become brittle, less common for whole houses.

Both PEX and copper are accepted for supply lines when installed correctly. The plumber explains which fits your house, the wall access and the budget. For a partial repipe, only the worst run is replaced, such as the kitchen and bath branch, and the rest is left.

A plumber may also look at the pressure regulator and the water heater connections at the same time, because new pipe is a good moment to fix them. See water heater replacement when the heater is old too.

Repiping an older Bayonne house or building.

Older buildings often have galvanized supply pipe, and the corrosion is hidden in the walls. The plumber can check pipe at the meter, in the basement and at fixture connections to judge what runs through the walls without opening everything.

Veolia has committed to spending at least $157 million on Bayonne's water system. That is for the system outside your building. The pipes inside are yours, and old inside pipe wears out on its own schedule.

Ask the Bayonne building department at 630 Avenue C what permits a repipe needs. Rules vary by project. If you own a multi-family house, plan which units are affected and how water will be shut off for each.

Insurance note.

Replacing worn pipe is a maintenance cost, not an insured event. Insurers may cover water damage from a sudden burst, but not the pipe itself. Keep records of leaks and repairs.

What to do if an old pipe fails before a repipe.

  • Close the main valve at once and open a low tap to drain the lines..
  • Keep away from wet outlets and appliances, and move belongings off the floor..
  • Do not wrap a failing pipe and then forget it. A patch is a short-term answer..
  • Photograph the failure, then call. A repeat failure is a reason to price a repipe..
  • Tell the plumber how many leaks you have had and where..
