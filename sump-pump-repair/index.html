Sump pump failure: water rising in the basement.

A sump pump sits in a pit in the basement floor and pumps groundwater out through a pipe to the outside. When it fails, the pit overflows and water spreads across the floor. The usual causes are a stuck float switch, a clogged intake, a dead motor, a power outage or a check valve that has failed.

Call the emergency line when water is rising and the pump is silent, running without moving water or cycling constantly. Unplug the pump if you can reach it dry, and keep out of standing water near outlets. A battery backup can keep water out during a power loss, and we can discuss that on the visit.

Pump does not start.

A stuck float, a tripped breaker or a dead motor leaves the pit full.

Runs but does not pump.

A clogged intake or a failed impeller spins without moving water.

Cycles constantly.

A bad check valve lets water flow back into the pit.

Loud or burning smell.

A worn motor or bearings is close to failing.

Pump repair, replacement and a backup plan.

The plumber tests the float, the motor and the discharge, then clears debris and replaces the failed part. If the pump is old or under-sized, replacement is more reliable. A sump pump is only as good as its power, so a battery backup or a water alarm is a sensible add-on.

Sump pumps near Newark Bay and the Kill Van Kull.

Bayonne is a peninsula with Newark Bay on the west and the Kill Van Kull on the south. Ground near the water can hold groundwater, and a basement there can take on water in heavy rain. The city's own 2023 notice for the 63rd Street Pumping Station places that site in the 100-year floodplain on the preliminary flood map.

Bayonne has also posted notices for a new relief sewer and underground storage at Cottage Street Park to reduce flooding. Those projects target street flooding, so keep your pump working in the meantime. Do not connect your sump discharge to the sanitary sewer unless the code allows it, and ask the building department at 630 Avenue C what applies.

Check whether your property sits in a flood zone through the city or FEMA map, and ask your insurer about flood coverage, which is separate from a standard homeowners policy.
