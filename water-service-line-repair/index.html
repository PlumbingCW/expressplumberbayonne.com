Service line leak or break: signs you cannot ignore.

A service line runs underground from the water main to the meter and the building. It can be copper, galvanized steel, plastic or, in older properties, lead. Over time it corrodes, cracks from ground movement or is damaged by digging, and the leak may never reach the surface.

Call the emergency line when pressure drops suddenly, when you see a wet patch, sinkhole or running water over the line, when the meter spins with everything off, or when the water goes out completely. Close the main valve inside to limit damage and note when it started.

Pressure that fell suddenly.

A break near the street drops pressure at every fixture.

Wet or soft ground.

Water reaches the surface as a damp patch or a sunken spot over the line.

Meter that keeps moving.

With all taps off, a moving meter means water is escaping.

Dirty or discolored water.

A break can let soil in and stir rust loose.

The service line is regulated piping. According to the state plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, applies, and a new connection or a street opening needs city approval. Veolia operates the Bayonne water system, so ask where the owner's line ends and the utility's begins.

In Hoboken, the owner generally owns the line from the main to the meter, so a service line problem there follows a different path.

Low water pressure: one fixture or the whole house.

Low pressure at one tap is a clogged aerator or a worn valve. Low pressure at every tap points to the service line, the pressure regulator or the main. The plumber checks the pressure with a gauge at a hose spigot, then follows the pipe back toward the street to find the restriction.

Pattern.Likely cause.Next step.
One tap weak.Aerator or cartridge.Clean or replace.
Hot only is weak.Water heater or hot line.See water heater repair.

Sudden drop with wet ground.Service line break.Isolate and repair.
Slowly worsening.Corroded pipe.Plan replacement.

Hidden leaks cause pressure loss too. A leak detection visit can separate a service line leak from a leak in a wall.

Replacement, repair and trenchless options.

If the failure is one joint or a short section, the plumber digs to it and replaces that piece. If the pipe is corroded along its length, replacing the whole line costs more now and avoids repeated digs. Some lines can be replaced with a trenchless method that pulls a new pipe along the old path through small access pits.

The plumber finds the path, checks for other utilities and tells you what is needed before digging. Work near the curb or in the street is different from work on private property, and the utility and the city may have to be involved.

Burst pipes inside the building are a different job. See burst pipe repair for those.

Water lines in Bayonne: Veolia, the meter and the street.

All of Bayonne's water comes from the North Jersey Water Supply District through an aqueduct that Veolia has described as about 100 years old. Veolia operates the system for the city, which keeps ownership. Veolia has said it will invest at least $157 million in the system over the life of its agreement.

Ask Veolia where its responsibility ends and yours begins on your service line, and whether your line material is on record. In January 2026 a damaged 30-inch main in South Kearny caused a loss of pressure and a boil water advisory from January 22 to January 24. A pressure event like that can stir sediment and shake loose weak fittings, so check for new leaks afterward.

Veolia says its latest lead and copper samples were under the EPA action level. That is a system result and not a guarantee for your pipes. If you do not know what your service line is made of, ask Veolia and have the plumber check at the meter.
