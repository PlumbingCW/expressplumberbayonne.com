What counts as plumbing installation.

Installation work includes new sinks, toilets, tubs and showers, kitchen and bath remodels, washer and dishwasher hookups, outdoor spigots, utility sinks and new supply or drain lines for an added bathroom. New construction and additions call for rough-in plumbing before walls close, then trim after.

Good installation work starts with a plan: where each line enters, how drains slope, where the shutoffs go and where a cleanout is needed. Shutoffs at every fixture and a labeled main make the next repair faster. Our faucet and sink repair page covers the repair side.

Remodels.

Moving a sink or a toilet changes supply and drain lines, and the plumber plans venting and slope.

Appliance hookups.

Washers, dishwashers and ice makers need correct supply, drain and shutoff.

New construction.

Rough-in plumbing is set before walls close and inspected before cover-up.

Additions.

A new bathroom needs supply, drain, vent and a path to the main stack.

New fixtures and appliances fall under the state plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which adopts a published standard with New Jersey changes. Gas connections add safety rules. PSE&G says to leave the building and call if you ever smell gas.

Water softener and filtration systems.

Hard water leaves scale on fixtures, heaters and appliances. A softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium, and filters remove chlorine taste, sediment or other contaminants. Which one you need depends on your water and what bothers you.

Do not guess the hardness. Ask Veolia for its annual water quality report for Bayonne, and test your tap. After the January 2026 boil water advisory, the city told people with softeners and filters to run them through a regeneration cycle, or follow the maker's steps, so both need upkeep.

Softeners and filters connect to the main supply with bypass valves and a drain line for the regeneration water. The plumber sets the connections, tests for leaks and shows you how to bypass the unit.

Gas appliance connections: safety first.

Gas water heaters, ranges, dryers and boilers need correct gas pipe sizing, shutoffs, flexible connectors and tested joints. Gas work belongs to a licensed professional, and the lines are pressure tested before use.

If you smell gas.

Leave the building at once, move away, then call PSE&G at 1-800-880-7734 or 911 from outside. Do not use switches, phones or lighters inside. Call a plumber only after the utility says it is safe.
