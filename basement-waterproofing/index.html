Why basements get wet and what plumbing can fix.

Basement water comes from three places: groundwater pressing through walls and floors, surface water that drains toward the house and water from the plumbing system itself. A plumber first rules out leaks and sewer backup, because the fix is different for each.

Plumbing-based waterproofing gives groundwater somewhere to go. An interior drain along the footing collects water and sends it to a sump pit, and a pump moves it outside. A backwater valve on the sewer line stops sewage from flowing back in during heavy storms.

Damp walls and floor.

White mineral staining or a damp patch at the base of the wall points to seepage.

Water after storms.

Puddles on the floor after hard rain suggest groundwater or surface runoff.

Musty smell.

Persistent damp encourages mold and rot.

Sewage in floor drains.

That is a sewer problem and calls for sewer line cleaning or a backwater valve.

Waterproof cement and coatings can slow minor damp on the inside of a wall, but they do not stop water pressure from outside. A coating over a wall that is under hydrostatic pressure will often fail. A drain and pump deal with the cause.

What to do when the basement takes on water.

  • Stay out of standing water if outlets, the panel or appliances are in it. Leave the power off until an electrician says it is safe..
  • Move stored items, rugs and boxes up off the floor..
  • Take photos and video before cleanup for your insurer or landlord..
  • Run a dehumidifier and fans once the water is gone to limit mold..
  • Call a plumber if water returns after dry days, because the source may be a pipe..
