When a clogged drain is an emergency.

Drain cleaning means clearing a blockage so wastewater flows freely again. The plumber uses a hand cable, a drain machine with cutting heads, or high-pressure water, depending on where the clog sits and what it is made of.

One slow sink can usually wait for a same-day visit. Call the emergency line when water is rising toward the rim, when two or more fixtures back up together, when the toilet gurgles after the tub drains, or when sewage shows in a floor drain. Those signs point to the main line, not a single trap.

Kitchen sink.

Grease, soap film and food scraps build up in the trap and the horizontal run behind the wall.

Shower or tub.

Hair and soap form a mat just below the strainer, then slow the whole drain.

Floor drain.

Debris and dried sediment settle in basement and laundry drains, then push back during heavy use.

Main line.

Several fixtures slow or gurgle at once, and the lowest drain in the house backs up first.

New Jersey's plumbing subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, adopts the IAPMO National Standard Plumbing Code Illustrated/2021 with state amendments. Cleaning an existing drain rarely needs a permit, but cutting out or replacing pipe can. Ask the Bayonne building department at 630 Avenue C.

Kitchen sink clogs and drain snaking.

Kitchen drains clog more than any other line in the house. Cooking fat cools inside the pipe, collects food particles and narrows the passage. A cable machine breaks through the blockage, and a second pass with a cutting head scrapes the pipe wall so the clog does not return in a month.

Drain snaking works well for hair, paper and soft debris. When the cable only punches a hole through grease or roots, hydro jetting washes the full pipe diameter clean. If the same line clogs repeatedly, a camera inspection shows whether a sag, crack or root is the real cause.

A drain line that leaks into a wall or crawl space shows up differently. You may see a damp stain, a musty smell or a gurgle with no clog. That needs a camera and a repair, not more cabling.
