Should I get a sewer scope inspection?

A sewer scope is a camera inspection of the private pipe between your building and the street connection. The plumber feeds a waterproof camera through a cleanout, watches the live image, records the run and notes the distance to each problem. The video becomes a record you can keep.

The scope is most useful in three cases. Buyers use it before closing on an older house. Owners use it after a line has clogged more than once. Contractors use it before relining or replacing pipe, so the scope of the repair is clear before work begins.

Before you buy.

A seller's house can look perfect while the main line holds roots or a collapse. A scope shows it before closing.

After repeat clogs.

If cabling gives only temporary relief, a defect is usually the reason.

Before a repair.

The camera shows the length, depth and number of defects so the repair is priced correctly.

After a repair.

A second recording shows the repair is clear and the pipe flows.

According to the Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, repairs need a licensed plumber. An inspection is a look, not a repair, so it needs no permit. The results may point to repairs that do. The State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers says plumbing is for master plumbers and authorized contractors, so use the video to get priced repairs from licensed plumbers.

What the camera finds and how to read it.

Finding.What it looks like.What it means.
Root intrusion.Fine white or brown roots at joints.Joint gap, often repairable by lining.
Offset joint.Pipe ends do not line up.Debris catches here, repair may be needed.
Belly or sag.Standing water in the pipe.Pipe has settled, waste collects.
Crack or fracture.Line across the pipe wall.Leak into soil, repair likely.
Scale or corrosion.Rough, built-up inner wall.Cleaning or replacement of old pipe.
Collapse.Pipe closed or flattened.Replacement section needed.

Not every finding needs a dig. A rough pipe may only need hydro jetting. A cracked section may be lined. The plumber explains each result on the video and what the options cost before any work starts.

Home buyers in Bayonne: why the scope matters.

Bayonne is a peninsula between Newark Bay and the Kill Van Kull, and the sewer system is operated by Veolia for the city. The line between a house and the street connection is generally the owner's to repair. A buyer who skips the scope can inherit a repair bill that the seller never mentioned.

Ask the seller where the cleanout is and whether the line has had work done. If there is no cleanout, tell the plumber, who may need to pull a toilet to feed the camera. The building department at 630 Avenue C can tell you what records exist for the property.

For a home near the water, such as Bergen Point or Port Johnson, ask the plumber to note the pipe slope and any standing water. A line that holds water in a basement-level run can back up in heavy rain.

What affects the price of a sewer inspection.

Cleanout accessA working cleanout is the fastest way in. Without one, the plumber may need another entry point.
Line length and bendsA long run with turns takes more time to record.
Cleaning firstA heavily coated pipe may need cleaning so the camera can see.
Report and videoA recorded video and notes are part of a useful inspection.

Insurance note.

A scope does not cover repairs. If you are buying, ask your agent whether the policy covers the sewer line or only backup damage.
